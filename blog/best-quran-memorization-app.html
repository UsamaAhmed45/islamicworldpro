Best Hifz App: How to Memorise the Qur'an with Your Phone

Islamic World Pro·Aurevia Solution·29 September 2026·9 min read

Memorising the Qur'an is built on two things: new lessons (sabaq) and constant revision (muraja'ah). Most people who stop do so because revision falls behind. A good Hifz app's real job is to keep revision on track — and to check you honestly when no teacher is listening.

Short answer: the best Hifz app lets you test yourself on real Mushaf pages with lines hidden, listens to your recitation and marks mistakes word by word, and schedules revision with spaced repetition so weak ayahs come back before you forget them. That is exactly what Islamic World Pro's Hifz Mode does.

How Hifz works — and where it breaks down

The traditional method is simple: learn a small portion, recite it to a teacher, and keep revising everything you have already memorised. The difficulty is scale. After a few juz, it is hard to know which pages are strong and which are slipping. Without a system, revision becomes random, and random revision lets weak pages fade.

The features that actually help

1. Real 16-line Mushaf pages

Huffaz remember the Qur'an visually — where an ayah sits on the page. Practising on the same 16-line layout keeps that visual memory intact.

2. Line masking with tap-to-check

Hide lines and recite from memory, then tap to reveal and check. You choose how much is hidden, from a single line to the whole page.

3. Voice recitation checking

Islamic World Pro listens while you recite and highlights mistakes word by word, revealing the text at the pace of your voice. It is not a replacement for a teacher, but it means every revision session is checked — not just the ones you recite to someone.

4. Spaced repetition (SM-2)

Spaced repetition schedules each portion for review just before you are likely to forget it. Strong pages come back less often; weak ones come back sooner. It turns revision from a growing debt into a manageable daily plan.

5. Weak-ayah detection

The app tracks where you stumble and surfaces those ayahs, so you spend time where it is needed.

6. Mutashabihat finder

Similar verses (mutashabihat) are the most common source of mix-ups for huffaz. Seeing them side by side makes the differences stick.

7. Streaks and a daily plan

A clear daily target and a streak that reflects real practice keep you consistent — and a home-screen widget reminds you of today's plan.

A simple daily routine with the app

  1. After Fajr — new lesson. Listen to the portion ayah by ayah, then read it with Tajweed colours until it flows.
  2. Test with masking. Hide the lines and recite with voice checking until you get it clean.
  3. Revision from the schedule. Complete the pages spaced repetition gives you for today, starting with weak ayahs.
  4. Before sleep — quick review of today's new lesson.
  5. Weekly — recite to a teacher or a friend who is a hafiz.

An app supports memorisation; it does not replace a qualified teacher who can correct your Tajweed and give you an ijazah.
